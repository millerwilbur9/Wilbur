The answer

No ready-made product does everything on your list. Don't buy one hoping it will.

I found nothing built for Jobber that lets you lock in your own close-rate formula, separates Direct from Contractor sales, catches archived and deleted quotes, and emails each rep a daily report. Every product that claims a Jobber integration covers part of the list. Some cover a small part.

  1. Fix two data habits first (this week, free). No software can fix them for you. See "What your Jobber data shows" below.
  2. Test the cheap options for 2 weeks: Jobber's own Custom Reports and Sales Pipeline, plus Crew Insight. If one gets an October rep-by-rep close rate exactly right, you may not need anything else.
  3. If neither passes (likely), pay a Jobber specialist to build a reporting database you own, with a set price: Jobber → database → Looker Studio dashboards. Expect $5,000–$15,000 to build and roughly $150–$500/month for hosting and support. It's the only option that meets every requirement, and it's what the Jobber API pushes anyone doing serious reporting toward.
  4. Add AccuInsight ($29/mo) for follow-up if Hannah needs a list of unanswered quotes before the custom build is done.

You said you'd rather buy than build. I agree with that rule, but here it costs you money: no product exists that meets your requirements. The middle path is to hire the build, own the result, and write a support agreement into the contract.

Why your Sheet keeps breaking

It isn't bad luck. The tools feeding it can't see everything that happens to a quote.

  • Zapier only sees three quote events: new quote, quote sent, quote approved. It never sees a quote being archived, edited or deleted. Verified (Zapier's Jobber guide). Anything built on Zapier, including Airtable through Zapier, has the same blind spot.
  • Jobber's webhooks (instant notices sent when a record changes) can send the same notice twice, contain only an ID, and get switched off if your receiver is slow or throws errors. Jobber publishes no retry schedule. Verified (Jobber webhook docs, Hookdeck guide). That means anything relying only on webhooks will eventually miss changes.
  • The Jobber API caps how fast you can pull data: a budget of 10,000 points that refills at 500 per second, plus 2,500 requests per 5 minutes. Verified (Jobber rate limits). One integrator measured a single dashboard load at 13,000–20,000 points Vendor claim (Adeocode). So dashboards can't read Jobber live. They need their own copy of the data.
  • Google Sheets can't stop duplicates. Nothing in a Sheet forces one row per Jobber quote, so duplicates and blank rows creep in.

What any dependable system has to do: (1) record webhooks so changes show up fast, (2) re-check every quote against Jobber each night to catch misses, archives and deletes, (3) store everything in a real database where each Jobber ID can appear only once, and (4) alert someone when a sync fails. If a vendor can't explain how they do all four, don't buy it.

Ready-made vs. custom

Ready-made (Jobber native, Crew Insight, AccuInsight)

  • Cheap and quick to start
  • Someone else maintains it
  • Uses their definitions, not yours
  • You can't see how they handle archived, deleted or reversed quotes, and with your data those cases matter
  • If the company shuts down or changes direction, your reporting goes with it

Custom database + dashboard tool

  • Costs more up front
  • Your formula, divisions and history, all in your control
  • Only as reliable as the builder and the support agreement
  • Moves with you if you ever leave Jobber
  • It's what the Jobber API's own limits push you toward

What bigger Jobber companies do: I found no published case study of a $10M Jobber company's reporting setup, so this part is inference. The integrators who serve larger Jobber shops (Adeocode and the Power BI firms) all describe the same thing: copy Jobber into a separate database in the background and put a dashboard tool on top. Companies that outgrow that usually move to ServiceTitan, which has close-rate reporting built in.

Write your rules down before anyone builds anything

Give this to every vendor and builder. If their numbers don't match these rules, they fail.

Won
The quote has an approval date, or its status is Approved or Converted. A quote that was approved and later archived still counts as won.
Open
Awaiting response or Changes requested, and never approved.
Lost
Marked lost in the Sales Pipeline, or archived without ever being approved.
Left out
Drafts, $0 quotes (for example #1850), deleted quotes, and anything you exclude by hand with a written reason.
Which date
The date the quote was presented: the sent date, or the approval date if it was closed at the table without being sent. Never the date the quote was created.
Close rate
Won ÷ (Won + Open + Lost) for quotes presented in the period. This is your formula, with clear definitions.
Division
Decide how a quote gets labeled Direct or Contractor: a Jobber client tag, a custom field, or the client type. Use one rule. Hannah sets it at intake.
Stale rule
Any quote awaiting response for 45 days with no reply gets archived as lost, with a reason. Keep Hannah's Thursday recovery calls going, but stop counting these as open quotes.

Implementation and migration plan

This week
Clean up and define. Agree on the rules above with Elmer. Clear out the 100+ stale quotes: archive the dead ones with a reason and move the live ones to Lead Recovery. Make sure every quote has a salesperson and every client has a lead source and division. Find out which Jobber plan you're on.
Weeks 1–2
Run the trials side by side. Have Jobber Custom Reports and Crew Insight each produce the October close rate by rep and by division. Compare both to a hand count of 20 quotes. Pass means exact matches. If one passes, stop there and add AccuInsight if you want a follow-up list.
Weeks 3–8
Hire the build (if the trials fail). Fixed price. Your rules go into the contract. Requirements: nightly re-check, failure alerts to you by email, documentation, you own the code and the database account, and a 12-month support agreement. Include importing all past quotes back to your first Jobber quote.
Weeks 9–12
Run both for 30 days. Keep the Sheet going alongside the new system. Every Friday, compare 10 random quotes. Fewer than 1 in 200 mismatches, with each one explained, means sign-off.
Cutover
Turn off the Sheet's sync and make the Sheet read-only. Keep it as an archive. Turn on the daily per-rep emails. Jackson and every future rep see their own dashboard; you and Elmer see the whole company.

Questions to ask each provider before you buy

  1. When a quote is archived, deleted or edited in Jobber, how long until your system shows it? Show me.
  2. Do you use webhooks, scheduled pulls, or both? How often do you re-check everything?
  3. If a sync fails, who finds out, and how? Do you fill in missed data automatically?
  4. Do you count Converted quotes as won? What about a quote approved at the table that was never sent?
  5. Can I set my own close-rate formula, and lock it so it's the same on every report?
  6. How do you split Direct from Contractor? Which Jobber field do you use?
  7. How far back do you import history? Is that included in the price?
  8. How do you prevent duplicate records? What's the unique key?
  9. Can each rep get their own report by email every morning?
  10. Can I add a deal by hand that isn't in Jobber? How is it labeled?
  11. What happens to my data if I cancel? Can I export all of it?
  12. Which Jobber plan do I need for your tool to work?
  13. What's the total cost at 3 reps, 6 reps and 10 reps?
  14. Give me two Jobber customers with more than $2M in revenue I can call.
  15. (For builders) Who owns the code and the database account? What does the support agreement cover, and how fast do you respond?
  16. (For builders) What happens when Jobber changes its API?
